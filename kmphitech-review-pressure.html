The review-pressure issue

One of the most concerning parts of my experience was the way reviews became part of the project process.

At points during the project, I was asked to provide a good or positive review while there was still substantial work remaining. The pattern I experienced was that work which had remained incomplete up to that point would receive more attention around a deadline or review stage, followed by assurances that the remaining work would be taken care of.

“Do the review and we will take care of it.” This wording reflects my recollection of the assurance I received. The original Fiverr message and its date should be displayed alongside this statement where the original record is available.

The concern for me was not simply being asked for a review. It was the combination of review pressure, order completion and unfinished work.

What I documented to Kmphitech's founders

On 14 April 2026, I wrote directly to the founders because I believed the project had reached a point where their involvement was necessary. In that message I described a recurring pattern in which there was more apparent attention when a deadline was approaching or when a review was needed.

“there is only more care when the deadline is approaching, or there is a need for review.” Statement from my 14 April 2026 escalation to Kalpesh and Mithul, describing my experience.

I also told them that, in my experience of the phased project structure, reviews could appear strongly positive while the customer was still depending on the developer for the smooth continuation of later phases. I specifically raised my concern that after the first two phases, a substantial portion of the overall investment had already been funded.

Important distinction: These statements are my account of what I experienced and what I told the company. They are not presented as proof of the company's internal intentions or as a claim about every Kmphitech customer.

Why completing a Fiverr order can change the customer's position

A customer who is inexperienced with Fiverr may reasonably believe that a stage has finally been delivered and that the remaining problems will be resolved as promised.

  1. A stage or order is brought to completion after significant delay.
  2. The customer is asked for a positive review or encouraged to review the work.
  3. The customer gives the review in good faith because they have been assured that remaining work will be handled.
  4. The Fiverr order becomes completed.
  5. The customer continues waiting for outstanding work or fixes.
  6. The normal cancellation window can pass while the work is still being resolved.
  7. The customer has already invested substantial money, time and effort into the project.
  8. Starting over with another developer can become difficult because of the money already invested and the project knowledge tied to the existing developer.

This can create a sunk-cost effect: after investing heavily in a project, the customer may feel that continuing is easier than starting again.

What Fiverr's current policy says

Fiverr's published review guidance addresses attempts to manipulate feedback, including attempts to obtain favorable reviews through inappropriate pressure or by tying delivery or other benefits to a particular rating. See the Fiverr reviews and content guidelines.

Fiverr's payment terms also describe a normal cancellation period for certain completed orders or milestones and note that exceptional post-window cancellations can sometimes be considered by Customer Support. See the Fiverr Payment Terms.

My warning to other customers: Do not treat a promise to fix outstanding work after completion as equivalent to having the work completed. Before accepting completion or giving a review, make sure you understand exactly what has been delivered, what remains outstanding, and what protection you still have through the platform.

I therefore would not say that “after 14 days you can do absolutely nothing.” The more accurate point is that once the normal cancellation period has passed, the customer's position can become substantially weaker, although exceptional remedies may still exist.

Kmphitech's explanation

For balance, I have also recorded the company's explanation rather than only my own account.

In its 14 April 2026 response, Kmphitech stated that the initial June, July and August milestones were completed within the agreed timelines with one four-day extension. The company said substantial later time was spent on discussions, refinements and iterations concerning KPI calculations and logic, which it said required multiple revisions based on evolving inputs.

The company further stated that the calculation logic was highly specific to the application and that changes affected multiple areas including the homepage and calculation modules. It characterized the latest calculation changes as appearing to introduce a new level of modification and proposed implementing the final confirmed calculation logic within 1–2 working days.

Why this is included: A credible project review should let the reader see both the customer's account and the developer's explanation. The disagreement over scope and KPI logic is documented separately from the review pressure issue.

Its listed scope included multiple farms and ponds, role-based access, farming-cycle initialization, four daily feeding slots, feed tracking, biomass tracking, sampling records, FCR calculation, growth monitoring and related reporting, together with Android, iOS, web-panel, backend, testing and deployment work.

The problems did not end at store publication

The applications were eventually submitted to the stores, but the project record did not stop there.

In August 2026 I reported a production KPI problem affecting closed farming cycles. Later, on 26 September 2026, I reported another production problem that I had discovered while actually using the application. On 27 September the backend team identified a string-versus-number comparison problem involving time-field data and said it had been corrected. On 29 September the latest record available to me still showed the Play Console build approved while the App Store update was waiting for review.

This matters to the overall review because “published” was not the same thing as the end of the project experience.

Conclusion

My concern is not that a developer asked a customer to leave feedback. My concern is the combination of unfinished work, assurances about future completion, review requests, order completion and the resulting reduction in the customer's practical refund options.

That experience is why I believe customers commissioning a large software project through Fiverr should be extremely careful about accepting an order as complete simply because the developer says the remaining work will be handled afterwards.

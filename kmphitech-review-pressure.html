Kmphitech Review: Review Requests, Order Completion & Outstanding Work

This page documents a separate part of my experience with Kmphitech LLP: requests for good or positive reviews while project work remained outstanding, assurances that remaining work would be handled, and the practical consequences I experienced after Fiverr orders were completed.

The issue I want other customers to understand

One of the most concerning parts of my experience was the way reviews became part of the project process.

At points during the project, I was asked to provide a good or positive review while there was still substantial work remaining. The pattern I experienced was that work which had remained incomplete up to that point would be pushed forward around the review or completion stage, followed by assurances that the remaining work would be taken care of.

“Do the review and we will take care of it.” This wording reflects my recollection of the assurance I received. Where the original Fiverr message is available, it should be published alongside the screenshot and date.

The concern for me was not simply being asked for a review. It was the combination of review pressure, order completion and unfinished work.

Why this can put a customer in a difficult position

A customer who is inexperienced with Fiverr may reasonably believe that the stage has finally been delivered and that the remaining problems will be resolved as promised.

  1. A stage or order is brought to completion after significant delay.
  2. The customer is asked for a positive review or encouraged to review the work.
  3. The customer gives the review in good faith because they have been assured that remaining work will be handled.
  4. The Fiverr order becomes completed.
  5. The customer continues waiting for outstanding work or fixes.
  6. The normal cancellation window can pass while the work is still being resolved.
  7. The customer has already invested substantial money, time and effort into the project.
  8. Starting over with another developer becomes increasingly difficult because of the money already invested and the project knowledge tied to the existing developer.

This can create a powerful sunk-cost effect: after investing heavily in a project, the customer may feel that continuing is easier than starting again.

What Fiverr's current policies say

This part is important because the concern is not based only on my personal interpretation. Fiverr's published policies address review manipulation.

Fiverr says feedback manipulation includes attempts to artificially influence reviews, ratings or reputation. Its review policy also addresses attempts to condition delivery, final files, future benefits or refunds on receiving a particular review or rating outcome.

That does not by itself establish that a particular communication from Kmphitech LLP violated Fiverr's policy. The relevant evidence is the actual message and its context, which is why preserving the original Fiverr communications is important.

The Fiverr cancellation-window problem

There is another part of this situation that I believe future customers should understand before accepting an order as complete.

Fiverr's current Payment Terms state that completed orders or milestones may be cancelled following Customer Support review for certain qualifying circumstances, including potential misconduct or policy violations, within 14 days after an order or milestone is marked complete. Fiverr also states that, in rare circumstances, Customer Support may cancel a completed order even after 14 days.

My warning to other customers: Do not treat a promise to fix outstanding work after completion as equivalent to having the work completed. Before accepting completion or giving a review, make sure you understand exactly what has been delivered, what remains outstanding, and what protection you still have through the platform.

I therefore would not say that “after 14 days you can do absolutely nothing.” The more accurate point is that once the normal cancellation period has passed, the customer's position can become substantially weaker, although exceptional remedies may still exist.

What I can and cannot conclude

What I can say: I am documenting my own experience, the communications I received, the project timeline and the consequences I experienced after orders were completed.

What I cannot establish from my experience alone: I cannot prove that there was an intentional company-wide scheme, that every customer was treated this way, or what the company's internal intentions were.

For that reason, I am deliberately describing this as my experience and the pattern I observed, rather than presenting an allegation about every Kmphitech customer or asserting an intention that I cannot independently prove.

Evidence to preserve

  • Original Fiverr messages asking for a review.
  • Any message requesting a positive or good review specifically.
  • Messages stating or implying that remaining work would be taken care of after the review.
  • Delivery timestamps and order-completion dates.
  • Messages showing what remained unfinished at the time of completion.
  • Later messages showing that promised remaining work was still outstanding.
  • Fiverr order pages and milestone records.

The strongest version of this page is one that lets readers inspect the original communication and compare it with the subsequent project timeline. Exact screenshots and message dates should therefore be added wherever they are available.

Conclusion

My concern is not that a developer asked a customer to leave feedback. My concern is the combination of unfinished work, assurances about future completion, review requests, order completion and the resulting reduction in the customer's practical refund options.

That experience is why I believe customers commissioning a large software project through Fiverr should be extremely careful about accepting an order as complete simply because the developer says the remaining work will be handled afterwards.
